Deep learning methods for dynamic economic models typically train a network to satisfy hand-derived equilibrium conditions, which converge to the wrong solution if a condition is derived incorrectly. I ask whether a reinforcement-learning method that uses only the reward and the laws of motion can instead recover the exact solution of a climate-economy model. In the Analytic Climate Economy with DICE 2016 and DICE 2023 functional forms, a model-based actor-critic that differentiates through the known dynamics recovers the optimal consumption and abatement paths, with a mean abatement error between 0.010 and 0.016 across six training runs, and the social cost of carbon implied by the gradient of its critic is within 1--4\% of the closed-form value. The welfare loss relative to the optimum is below 0.04\% of consumption. A critic trained only on values, however, fits the value function closely but implies a social cost of carbon with the wrong sign, and the policy stops abating. The social cost of carbon is a small gradient of a large function, and recovering it requires training the critic on the gradient of the Bellman equation as well (value-gradient learning), with each component normalized by its own scale. A correctly specified equilibrium-condition method remains about an order of magnitude more precise, but the reward-based method needs no derived conditions.
